01 · The Challenge

Improving delivery confidence before execution begins

Across a large Agile technology organization, delivery predictability depended on more than sprint execution. Teams needed clearer requirements, stronger technical alignment, and earlier visibility into dependencies and risks before committing to delivery.

Readiness

Inconsistent story readiness

Gaps in acceptance criteria, requirements clarity, and other Definition of Ready expectations could carry into sprint commitments, creating uncertainty during execution.

Alignment

Product and technology misalignment

Product, Architecture, and Engineering needed stronger alignment on requirements, solution implications, and technical considerations before work moved forward.

Dependencies

Risks identified too late

Dependencies and delivery risks needed to be surfaced earlier, with clear ownership and follow-through before they affected execution.

Impact

Uncertainty entering sprint execution

When readiness gaps, technical questions, and dependencies remained unresolved, teams had less confidence in their commitments and delivery plans.

html
02 · The Approach

Strengthening the delivery system—not just the sprint

I focused on improving the practices that influence delivery confidence before work enters execution: story readiness, cross-functional alignment, technical impact assessment, and proactive management of risks and dependencies.

01 · Readiness

Make readiness gaps visible

Established a more structured way to evaluate stories against Definition of Ready expectations, identify missing information, and address gaps during refinement and planning before teams committed to the work.

02 · Alignment

Connect Product, Architecture, and Engineering

Strengthened collaboration around requirements, solution considerations, and technical implications so teams could clarify assumptions and resolve questions earlier in the delivery lifecycle.

03 · Dependencies

Surface risks and establish ownership

Emphasized early identification of cross-team dependencies, delivery risks, and unresolved decisions—with explicit ownership and follow-through to reduce surprises during execution.

04 · Continuous Improvement

Use evidence to improve planning

Reinforced a delivery approach in which teams use visible readiness gaps, technical questions, and dependency status to make better-informed planning and commitment decisions.

Applied AI · A Practical Example

Operationalizing story readiness with an AI-assisted report

To make readiness assessment more structured and repeatable, I designed and built a Jira-integrated workflow using GitHub Copilot. A user selects a sprint through a simple interface, and a background process evaluates its stories against Definition of Ready criteria.

The resulting report identifies each story, its readiness status, and comments describing gaps. It also highlights one or two exemplary stories that score highly against the readiness criteria, giving teams concrete examples of what good readiness looks like.

The report supports—not replaces—team judgment. Its purpose is to help Product and Engineering address gaps during refinement and planning, before those gaps become execution problems.

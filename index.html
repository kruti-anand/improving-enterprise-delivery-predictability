01 · The Challenge

Improving delivery confidence before execution begins

Across a large Agile technology organization, delivery predictability depended on more than sprint execution. Teams needed clearer requirements, stronger technical alignment, and earlier visibility into dependencies and risks before committing to delivery.

Readiness

Inconsistent story readiness

Gaps in acceptance criteria, requirements clarity, and other Definition of Ready expectations could carry into sprint commitments, creating uncertainty during execution.

Alignment

Product and technology misalignment

Product, Architecture, and Engineering needed stronger alignment on requirements, solution implications, and technical considerations before work moved forward.

Dependencies

Risks identified too late

Dependencies and delivery risks needed to be surfaced earlier, with clear ownership and follow-through before they affected execution.

Impact

Uncertainty entering sprint execution

When readiness gaps, technical questions, and dependencies remained unresolved, teams had less confidence in their commitments and delivery plans.

Technical Solution Design · Applied AI

Turning story readiness into a repeatable, AI-assisted workflow

I identified an opportunity to make story readiness assessment more consistent and actionable. Rather than relying solely on manual review, I designed and built a Jira-integrated workflow using GitHub Copilot to help evaluate sprint stories against defined Definition of Ready criteria.

The workflow combines a simple sprint-selection interface with background processing that evaluates stories and produces a structured report. The report identifies each story, its readiness status, and specific gaps requiring attention. It also highlights exemplary stories to give teams concrete reference points for readiness expectations.

Solution design and delivery considerations:

  • Defined the evaluation framework: translated readiness expectations into criteria that could be applied consistently across sprint stories.
  • Designed the workflow: connected sprint selection, story evaluation, and report generation into a repeatable process.
  • Kept people accountable for decisions: positioned the output as decision support for Product and Engineering, not as a substitute for team judgment.
  • Focused on earlier intervention: made missing information and readiness gaps visible during refinement and planning, when teams could still address them.

The broader objective was to operationalize a delivery practice: turn qualitative readiness expectations into a structured, repeatable workflow that helps teams identify issues earlier and make better-informed execution decisions.

The leadership takeaway

I brought these practices together to strengthen delivery confidence, using AI where it could make the work more structured and repeatable.
